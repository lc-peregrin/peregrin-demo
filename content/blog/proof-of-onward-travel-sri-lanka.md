# Proof of Onward Travel for Sri Lanka: What You Actually Need in 2026

Sri Lanka is a small island that somehow contains about six different countries. You go for the beaches, and within a week you're on a blue train winding through tea country, eating hoppers for the third time that day, and quietly rearranging your flight home. It's a place that rewards staying longer than you planned.

Which is exactly why the onward-travel question deserves a minute of your attention before you go. Sri Lanka's entry system is mostly smooth, but the airline flying you in may want to see that you have a way out, and the entry authorisation has expectations too. So here's what proof of onward travel really means for Sri Lanka, who actually asks, and how to have it sorted before you're at a check-in desk with your flip-flops already in holiday mode.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Sri Lanka

When Sri Lanka lets you in, it's granting you a stay tied to your entry authorisation. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you plan to leave within that window: a confirmed booking showing you departing the island, home or onward to your next stop, before your permitted stay ends.

It's separate from the authorisation itself. Your entry approval says you're allowed in. Your onward booking says you have a plan to leave. For a tourist trip, both can come up, one when you apply or arrive, the other when you're standing at the airline counter before you've even left home.

## The entry picture, briefly

Most visitors arrive on Sri Lanka's electronic entry authorisation, generally known as the **ETA** (Electronic Travel Authorization), applied for online before you fly. It's typically issued for short tourist stays with the option to extend once you're in the country, and there are separate categories for business and transit.

Here's the honest part. The specifics, stay length, fees, which nationalities are eligible, and even the exact name and process, get updated, and there have been changes to how Sri Lanka runs and prices the system in recent years. So treat any number you read online, including here, as a prompt to go and verify rather than gospel. Confirm your own eligibility and permitted stay on the official Sri Lankan government ETA portal before you book anything, and apply only on that official site. Plenty of lookalike sites copy it and add a fee for nothing.

Whatever the current details, the onward-travel logic holds: your departure booking should show you leaving within the stay you've been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they're not the same

This trips people up, so it's worth splitting "will I get asked?" into its two real answers.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that surprises people. Airlines carry the cost, and often a fine, if they fly someone to Sri Lanka who's then refused entry. So the check-in agent is protecting the airline, not enforcing Sri Lankan law. They may ask to see a return or onward booking before handing you a boarding pass, and an approved ETA on your phone doesn't always end the conversation. No onward proof, and you can find yourself unable to board a flight you've already paid for.

**At Sri Lankan immigration, on arrival.** Less predictable, more human. An officer can ask about your plans, and a clear answer backed by a booking makes a short chat shorter. It tends to come up if your stay is long, your answers are vague, or something about your trip invites the question.

The practical takeaway is the usual one: even if you reckon the immigration officer won't push, the airline might, so have something real ready to show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to Sri Lanka

1. **Confirm your entry route and permitted stay** for your nationality on the official Sri Lankan ETA portal.
2. **Apply for your ETA early** on the official government site, and save the approval to your phone. Don't leave it to the last day.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Match your dates.** Make sure your onward booking sits inside your entry window, and that names match your passport exactly.
5. **Keep everything accessible**: ETA approval and onward booking on your phone, plus a printout of the onward booking with its reference, ready before you reach the counter.

## FAQ

**Do I really need onward travel if my ETA is approved?**
Your ETA and your onward proof answer two different questions. The authorisation gives you permission to enter; the onward booking shows you plan to leave within it. Airlines in particular often want to see it, so yes, have onward travel ready even with an approved ETA.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A fully paid ticket you don't intend to use just ties up money for the same job.

**How long can I stay in Sri Lanka as a tourist?**
It depends on your nationality and the current ETA rules, which get updated, and short tourist stays can often be extended once you're in the country. Confirm your own allowance on the official portal, and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Where do I apply for the ETA?**
Only on the official Sri Lankan government ETA portal. Numerous third-party sites copy its look and charge a markup, so check the web address carefully before you enter any details.

**What if my plans past Sri Lanka genuinely aren't decided?**
That's the common case, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation is for. You get real, verifiable proof to show at the gate without committing to a flight and a date you're not ready to commit to.
