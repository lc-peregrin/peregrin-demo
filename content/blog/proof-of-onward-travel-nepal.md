# Proof of Onward Travel for Nepal: What You Actually Need in 2026

Nepal has a habit of stretching time. You arrive planning a tidy two weeks, do the maths on a trek, realise the weather window has other ideas, and next thing you know you're rebooking everything from a teahouse with a view that makes the admin feel very small. The mountains set the schedule here, not you, and that's most of the appeal.

The catch is that flexible plans and a check-in desk don't always see eye to eye. Nepal is welcoming to enter, visa on arrival covers a lot of travellers, but the airline flying you into Kathmandu has its own checklist, and one of the boxes is often "show me you're leaving." So here's what proof of onward travel really means for Nepal, who actually asks, and how to have it handled before you're at the counter watching a queue build.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Nepal

When Nepal grants you entry, it's giving you a defined stay tied to your visa. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you plan to leave inside that window: a confirmed booking showing you departing Nepal, whether flying home or continuing on to India, Bhutan, or elsewhere, before your permitted time is up.

It's separate from the visa itself. Your visa says you're allowed in. Your onward booking says you have a plan to leave. Nepal is a place people genuinely lose track of the calendar in, between treks and delayed mountain flights, so a clear exit booking reassures the airline that you're a visitor with a plan, not a one-way arrival.

## The visa picture, briefly

Most travellers enter Nepal on a **visa on arrival** at Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu (and at some land borders), available to many nationalities for tourism, typically in 15, 30, and 90-day options that you pay for on entry. A number of nationalities aren't eligible for visa on arrival and need to arrange a visa in advance, and Nepal also offers online pre-registration to speed up the arrival process.

Here's the honest part: which nationalities qualify, the stay lengths, the fees, and the annual limits on tourist days all get updated, and they depend on your specific passport. So treat any number you read online, including here, as a prompt to check rather than gospel. Confirm your own eligibility and permitted stay on the official Nepal Department of Immigration channels before you fly, and use only official sites for any pre-registration. Lookalike sites are common and love to add a fee for nothing.

Whichever route applies, the onward-travel logic holds: your departure booking should show you leaving within the stay you've been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they're not the same

This is where people get caught, so it helps to split "will I get asked?" into its two real answers.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that surprises travellers. If an airline flies you to Nepal and you're refused entry, the airline wears the cost of flying you back, often with a fine on top. So the check-in agent is protecting the airline, not enforcing Nepali immigration law. They may want to see a return or onward booking before printing your boarding pass, and "I'll get my visa on arrival" doesn't always settle it. No onward proof, and you can be left unable to board a flight you've already paid for.

**At Nepali immigration, on arrival.** Less predictable, more human. An officer processing your visa on arrival can ask about your plans, and a clear answer backed by a booking keeps a short interaction short. It's more likely to come up if your stay is long, your answers are vague, or something about the trip invites a second question.

The takeaway is the usual one: even if you're confident the immigration officer won't push, the airline might, so have something real ready to show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to Nepal

1. **Confirm your entry route and permitted stay** for your nationality, visa on arrival or visa in advance, on the official Nepal Department of Immigration channels.
2. **Pre-register online if offered**, keep the confirmation and the correct visa fee in cash ready, and save everything to your phone.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Match your dates and name.** Make sure your onward booking sits inside your entry window, and that the name matches your passport exactly.
5. **Keep everything accessible**: any pre-registration and your onward booking on your phone, plus a printout of the onward booking with its reference, ready before you reach the counter.

## FAQ

**Do I need onward travel if I'm getting a visa on arrival in Nepal?**
Visa on arrival and onward proof answer two different questions. The visa gives you permission to enter; the onward booking shows you plan to leave within your permitted stay. Airlines in particular often want to see it before you board the inbound flight, so yes, have onward travel ready even if you'll get your visa at the airport.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A fully paid ticket you don't intend to use just ties up money for the same job.

**How long can I stay in Nepal as a tourist?**
It depends on your nationality and the visa length you take, and there are annual limits on tourist days that get updated, so confirm your own allowance on official channels. Whatever the length, make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Can I show an overland exit to India instead of a flight?**
If you're genuinely leaving overland, that can be your onward plan, but it's harder to present as a single checkable booking. Airlines checking you in for the inbound flight are most comfortable with a clear, verifiable onward booking, so have something checkable ready even if you intend to cross by land.

**What if my plans past Nepal genuinely aren't decided?**
That's the common case, especially with treks and mountain weather rewriting dates, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation is for. You get real, verifiable proof to show at the gate without committing to a flight and a date you're not ready to lock in.
