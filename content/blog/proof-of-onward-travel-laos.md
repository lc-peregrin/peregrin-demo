# Proof of Onward Travel for Laos: What You Actually Need in 2026

Laos has a way of slowing everyone down, in the best sense. You land in Luang Prabang for the monks and the waterfalls, drift south on a slow boat or a bus that takes longer than it should, and by the time you're in Vientiane your onward plans have quietly dissolved into "we'll see." That's kind of the point of Laos. Nobody comes here for a tight schedule.

The trouble is, airlines and border officials don't really do "we'll see." Laos itself tends to be relaxed about visitors, but the airline that flew you in, and sometimes the immigration desk on the way through, wants a plan for how you're leaving. That gap between the country's easy pace and the paperwork's stricter expectations is where onward-travel stress usually shows up. So here's what proof of onward travel actually means for Laos, who's likely to ask for it, and how to have it ready without it costing you your loose itinerary.

## What proof of onward travel means for Laos

When Laos admits you, whether on a visa on arrival, an e-visa, or a visa-free arrangement depending on your passport, it's granting you a stay for a set number of days. Proof of onward travel is simply a booking that shows you leaving Laos, or continuing on, before that window closes.

It's a different thing from the visa itself. Your visa or entry stamp is permission to be in the country. Your onward booking is evidence that you have a plan to leave it. Both can matter on the same trip, just at different moments: one when a Lao immigration officer processes your arrival, and one when an airline agent decides whether to let you board a flight to Laos in the first place.

## The entry picture, briefly

Many nationalities can get a visa on arrival at Laos's main international airports and at a number of land border crossings, and an e-visa option exists too, arranged online before you travel. Some passports get visa-free entry for a set number of days. Which category you fall into, how many days you're granted, the fees involved, and which specific border crossings actually issue visas on arrival (not all of them do) can all vary and can change without much notice.

That's the honest bit. Treat any specific number you read here or elsewhere as a starting point, not a guarantee, and verify your own situation on the official Lao immigration and e-visa channels before you book anything. As with most e-visa systems, there are copycat websites that mimic the official portal and charge a markup for doing nothing extra, so double-check the web address before you hand over any details or payment.

Whatever the current specifics, the underlying logic doesn't change: your onward booking should show you departing Laos within whatever stay you've actually been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they work differently

People tend to lump these together, and that's usually where the confusion starts.

**At check-in, before you board your flight to Laos.** This is the one that trips travellers up. If an airline flies you into Laos and you're then refused entry for any reason, the airline is the one that has to fly you back, usually at its own cost, and sometimes with a fine on top. So the check-in agent asking about your onward plans isn't enforcing Lao law, they're protecting their airline from that exact bill. A visa on arrival doesn't always settle it either. Airlines can, and do, ask to see proof you're leaving before they'll issue a boarding pass, and if you can't show it, you can be turned away at your departure airport, nowhere near Laos.

**At Lao immigration, on arrival.** Generally straightforward, especially if you're on a visa on arrival with the paperwork in order. But officers can and do ask about onward plans, particularly if your intended stay looks long, your story is vague, or you've arrived on a one-way ticket with nothing to show for what comes after.

The takeaway is a familiar one: even if you suspect the immigration officer at Wattay or Luang Prabang airport won't blink, the check-in agent at your departure gate might, so carry something genuine to show either way.

## Step by step before you fly to Laos

1. **Check your entry route and permitted stay** for your specific nationality on the official Lao immigration and e-visa channels, and confirm whether visa on arrival, e-visa, or visa-free applies to you, and which border crossings actually offer it.
2. **Apply early if you're using the e-visa**, through the official government site only, and save your approval to your phone and a printed copy.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a genuine verifiable reservation, a real flight you intend to take, or a fully refundable ticket, showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Check the details match.** Your onward booking dates should sit inside your granted stay, and the name should match your passport exactly.
5. **Keep it all handy**: visa approval and onward booking saved on your phone, plus a printed copy of the booking with its reference, ready before you reach the check-in desk or the immigration counter.

## FAQ

**Do I need onward travel if my Laos visa or e-visa is already approved?**
Yes, generally, and it's worth having ready regardless. Your visa answers whether you're allowed in. Your onward booking answers whether you have a plan to leave. Airlines in particular tend to ask for the second one even when the first is sorted.

**Does a held reservation count, or do I need a ticket I've actually paid for?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay, not necessarily a paid-in-full ticket. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to satisfy exactly that, since it's checkable and real. A fully paid ticket for a flight you don't intend to take just ties up money doing the same job.

**How long can I stay in Laos as a tourist?**
It depends on your nationality and the entry route you use, and it's the kind of detail that changes. Confirm your own permitted stay on the official Lao immigration channels, and make sure your onward booking falls inside it.

**Where do I apply for a Laos e-visa?**
Only through the official Lao government e-visa portal. There are lookalike sites that copy the design and charge extra for nothing, so check the URL carefully and avoid third-party resellers.

**What if I genuinely don't know where I'm going after Laos?**
That's an extremely normal state of mind here, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation is for. You get a real, verifiable booking to show at check-in or immigration, without locking yourself into a flight and a date you're not ready to commit to.
