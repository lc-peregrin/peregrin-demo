# Proof of Onward Travel for the Maldives: What You Actually Need in 2026

The Maldives runs one of the most open entry policies on the planet: essentially every nationality can get a free 30-day tourist visa on arrival, no advance application needed. Which sounds like the sort of place where nobody checks anything, and that's exactly the trap. The visa on arrival is easy, but it comes with listed conditions, and official Maldivian immigration guidance is specific about them: a complete travel itinerary including confirmed return journey tickets, a prepaid confirmed booking at a registered tourist facility, and enough funds for your stay. Plus a traveller declaration (IMUGA) submitted online in the 96 hours before your flight.

Airlines flying into Malé know that list, and they check the parts they can check, your ticket out among them, before you board. Here's how it all fits together.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for the Maldives

The 30-day visa on arrival gives you permission to stay for up to 30 days, not indefinitely. Proof of onward travel is evidence of a confirmed booking out of the Maldives, home or onward somewhere else, inside that window.

The official phrasing leans on "confirmed return journey tickets" as part of a complete itinerary, and adds a wrinkle worth noticing: if you're continuing to a third country rather than flying home, you should also be able to show you meet the entry requirements for wherever you're heading next. In other words, an onward booking to a country you couldn't actually enter doesn't solve the problem.

## The entry picture, briefly (and hedged, because it moves)

Tourists of all nationalities are currently granted a free 30-day visa on arrival, provided they meet the conditions: a passport or travel document with a machine-readable zone and at least a month's validity, the confirmed itinerary and accommodation described above, sufficient funds, and the IMUGA traveller declaration completed online within 96 hours before arrival. Extensions beyond 30 days exist but are their own process.

These conditions are set by Maldives Immigration and can change, so treat this as a snapshot: check the official immigration site close to your travel date, submit IMUGA in the correct window, and make sure your onward booking falls inside your 30 days.

## Two checkpoints, and they behave differently

**At check-in, before you board.** Airlines delivering you to Malé are responsible for taking you back if you're refused entry, so they screen for the checkable items: passport validity, and on one-way bookings especially, a ticket out. Some carriers also ask to see your IMUGA confirmation. A missing onward booking at check-in for a Maldives flight is a common, avoidable way to lose a holiday's first day, or the holiday.

**At Maldivian immigration, on arrival.** Officers grant the visa on arrival after confirming you meet the conditions, and admissibility is explicitly their call at the desk. Most tourist arrivals with resort bookings sail through in minutes. But the officer is entitled to ask for the return or onward ticket, the accommodation confirmation and evidence of funds, and one-way travellers or vague plans invite exactly those questions.

## Step by step before you fly to the Maldives

1. **Check your passport** has a machine-readable zone and comfortably more than a month's validity.
2. **Confirm your accommodation** is a prepaid booking at a registered tourist facility (resorts, registered guesthouses and liveaboards qualify; keep the confirmation handy).
3. **Sort your onward travel:** a real flight you'll use, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation, showing you leaving inside your 30 days, to a country you're entitled to enter.
4. **Submit your IMUGA traveller declaration** on the official portal within 96 hours before arrival, and save the confirmation QR to your phone.
5. **Keep everything accessible:** passport, IMUGA confirmation, accommodation booking and onward booking reference together, with a printed copy of the onward booking for check-in.

## FAQ

**Do I really need an onward ticket if everyone gets a visa on arrival?**
Yes. The visa on arrival is granted subject to conditions, and confirmed return journey tickets are on the official list. The openness of the policy is about not needing advance paperwork; it isn't a waiver of the conditions at the desk.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
What's expected is a confirmed booking out of the Maldives inside your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet exactly that, and because it's real and checkable, it does the job without you paying for airfare you don't need yet.

**What is IMUGA and when do I fill it in?**
IMUGA is the Maldives' online traveller declaration, required for arrival (and departure). You submit it free on the official portal within 96 hours before your flight and show the confirmation on arrival. It asks for personal, travel and accommodation details, so have your bookings sorted before you fill it in.

**Does my accommodation really have to be prepaid?**
Official guidance asks for a prepaid confirmed booking at a registered tourist facility. In practice this is the norm for Maldives trips anyway; just make sure your confirmation shows the booking is paid and the property is a registered one.

**Can I stay longer than 30 days?**
Extensions of the tourist visa exist as a separate in-country process with their own conditions and fees. Don't rely on one when booking your onward travel; show a booking that fits your initial 30 days.
