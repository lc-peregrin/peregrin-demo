# Proof of Onward Travel for Vietnam: What You Actually Need in 2026

Vietnam has quietly become one of the easiest countries in the region to get into on paper. The e-visa is long, cheap, and done entirely online. And yet people still get pulled aside at check-in for their flight *to* Vietnam, passport in hand, valid visa on their phone, and get asked a question they weren't ready for: "Where's your onward ticket?"

That gap, generous visa rules but a strict airline check, is exactly where travellers trip up. So here's what proof of onward travel actually means for Vietnam, who gets asked, and the calm way to have it sorted before you're standing at a counter in a hurry.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Vietnam

When you enter Vietnam, whether on an e-visa or a visa exemption, you're given permission to stay for a set period. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you intend to leave within it: a confirmed booking showing you departing Vietnam, either home or onward to another country, before your permitted stay runs out.

It's a separate thing from your visa. Your visa says you're *allowed* in. Your onward booking says you have a *plan to leave*. Airlines and, occasionally, immigration want to see both.

## The visa picture, briefly

Most travellers now enter Vietnam on the **e-visa**, which in its current form can be issued for up to 90 days, single or multiple entry, and is applied for online before you fly. A number of nationalities also get a **visa exemption** for shorter stays. The specifics shift and depend heavily on your passport, so check your own nationality against the official Vietnamese immigration portal before you book anything, and always apply for the e-visa on the official government site rather than a lookalike that charges a markup.

Whichever route you're on, the same onward-travel logic applies: your departure booking should show you leaving within the window you've been given.

## Who actually gets asked

Two checkpoints, and they behave very differently.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that catches people. Airlines carry the cost if they fly you somewhere you're refused entry, so many will ask to see onward or return travel before issuing a boarding pass. It doesn't matter that your Vietnamese e-visa is perfectly valid, the airline is protecting itself against a scenario that has nothing to do with your paperwork being correct. No onward proof, and you can be standing at the departure airport unable to board.

**At Vietnamese immigration, on arrival.** Less consistent, but it happens. An officer can ask to see your departure plan, particularly if your stay is long or your answers are vague. It's quick to satisfy if you have something ready, and awkward if you don't.

The takeaway is the same one that applies almost everywhere: even if you're sure the immigration officer won't ask, the airline might, so have something real ready to show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to Vietnam

1. **Confirm your entry route** (e-visa or exemption) and your permitted stay for your nationality, on the official portal.
2. **Apply for your e-visa** early on the official government site if you need one, and save the approval to your phone.
3. **Sort your onward travel**, a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Keep everything accessible**, visa approval and onward booking on your phone, and ideally a printout of the onward booking with its reference, ready at check-in.

## FAQ

**Do I really need onward travel if my e-visa is valid?**
Your e-visa and your onward proof answer two different questions. The visa gets you permission to enter, the onward booking shows you plan to leave. Airlines in particular often want both, so yes, have onward travel ready even with a valid e-visa.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A paid ticket you don't intend to use just ties up money.

**How long can I stay in Vietnam?**
The e-visa can currently run up to 90 days, and some nationalities get shorter visa-exempt stays. It depends on your passport and the option you use, so confirm your own allowance before you book, and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Where do I apply for the e-visa?**
Only on the official Vietnamese government immigration portal. Plenty of third-party sites mimic it and add a fee, so check the URL carefully.
