# Proof of Onward Travel for Egypt: What You Actually Need in 2026

There's a particular kind of jet lag that hits you the first time you stand in front of the pyramids at Giza and realise they're just... there, at the edge of a busy Cairo suburb, like they've been waiting patiently for you to notice. Egypt does that a lot. One day you're haggling in Khan el-Khalili, the next you're floating down the Nile on a felucca, and a few days after that you're weighing up whether to bolt on Luxor, or Dahab, or a diving trip in the Red Sea that definitely wasn't in the original plan.

That open-endedness is part of the appeal, but it runs into a very specific snag: a check-in agent asking to see your onward booking before they'll hand you a boarding pass. Egypt is easy to enter for a lot of travellers, but the airline flying you there answers to a different set of rules, and the tidy answer to "when are you leaving?" is a confirmed booking showing you departing. Here's what proof of onward travel actually means for Egypt, who's likely to ask, and how to have it sorted well before you're at the counter.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Egypt

When Egypt lets you in, whether on a visa on arrival, an e-visa, or a visa arranged in advance, it's granting you permission to stay for a defined period. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you intend to leave inside that window: a confirmed booking showing you departing Egypt, whether home or on to your next country, before your permitted stay expires.

It's a different question from your entry permission. Getting a visa on arrival or an approved e-visa means Egypt has agreed to let you in. Your onward booking is what shows an airline, and occasionally an immigration officer, that you have a plan to leave. Airlines in particular treat this as their problem to manage, not Egypt's, and that's the bit worth understanding properly.

## The visa picture, briefly (and it does change)

Many nationalities, including the UK, the US, most of the EU, Canada, and Australia, can currently get an Egyptian visa on arrival at the airport, or apply for an e-visa online before travel, typically valid for a short tourist stay. Some nationalities need to arrange a visa in advance through an embassy instead, and the list of who qualifies for what, along with fees and permitted stay lengths, does get revised.

So treat any specific figure you read, including in this guide, as a starting point rather than a fact to lock in your plans around. Confirm your own nationality's requirement, the correct fee, and your permitted length of stay on Egypt's official visa portal or through an Egyptian consulate before you book anything. If you're applying for an e-visa, use the official government site only. Egypt's visa system has attracted more than its share of copycat sites that charge a markup for a service the real portal provides directly, so double check the URL before you pay anyone.

Whichever route applies to you, the onward-travel logic sits underneath it regardless: your departure booking should show you leaving within whatever stay you've actually been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they don't behave the same way

This is where the confusion usually creeps in, so it's worth separating "will someone ask?" into its two genuinely different answers.

**At check-in, before you even board.** This is the one that catches people out. If an airline carries you to Egypt and you're then refused entry, the airline is on the hook to fly you back, usually at its own cost, sometimes with a fine on top. That's precisely why the desk agent checking you in might ask for a return or onward booking before printing your boarding pass, regardless of what your visa situation looks like. It's not Egyptian law they're enforcing, it's their airline's exposure. No proof, and you can find yourself stuck at your departure airport, ticket paid for and going nowhere.

**At Egyptian immigration, on arrival.** Less consistent, more a matter of individual judgement. An officer might ask about your onward plans, particularly if your stay looks long or your answers are vague, and having a booking ready to show turns a possible hold-up into a non-event.

The practical upshot doesn't really change from country to country: even if you suspect the immigration side won't push you on it, the airline checking you in very well might, so have something real ready before you get anywhere near the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to Egypt

1. **Confirm your entry requirement** for your nationality, visa on arrival, e-visa, or advance visa, along with the correct fee and permitted stay, on Egypt's official visa portal or through a consulate.
2. **Apply for your e-visa early if that's your route**, through the official site only, and save the approval to your phone and as a printout.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a real flight you intend to take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Check your dates and your name.** Your onward booking needs to fall inside your permitted stay, and the name has to match your passport exactly, no nicknames, no shortcuts.
5. **Keep it all accessible**: visa approval (if applicable) and onward booking saved to your phone, plus a printed copy of the onward booking with its reference visible, ready before you reach the counter.

## FAQ

**Do I need proof of onward travel if I'm getting a visa on arrival in Egypt?**
Yes, generally. A visa on arrival answers the question "can I enter," not "do I plan to leave." Airlines checking you in for the flight to Egypt often ask for onward proof regardless of your visa route, so have it ready either way.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The actual requirement is a confirmed onward booking that falls within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference meets that requirement, since it's checkable the same way a paid ticket is. A fully paid ticket you're not planning to use just ties up your money for no extra benefit.

**How long can I stay in Egypt as a tourist?**
It depends on your nationality and the entry route you use, and these details do get updated from time to time. Confirm your own permitted stay on Egypt's official visa channels before you book anything, and make sure your onward travel shows you leaving inside it.

**Can I show a land border crossing instead of a flight booking?**
If you're genuinely leaving overland, say, on to Jordan via the Sinai, that can work in principle, but it's a less standard case and often harder to evidence cleanly. Airlines checking you in for your inbound flight are generally most comfortable with a clear, verifiable onward flight booking, so that's the safer thing to have ready.

**What if I genuinely don't know my route past Egypt yet?**
That's the normal situation for most travellers, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation solves. You get a real, checkable booking to show at the desk without locking yourself into a flight and a date before you're ready to commit.
