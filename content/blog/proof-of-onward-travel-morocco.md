# Proof of Onward Travel for Morocco: What You Actually Need in 2026

Morocco has a way of speeding up and slowing down at the same time. One hour you're haggling over a rug you didn't plan to buy while three cups of mint tea appear from nowhere, the next you're on a rooftop watching the call to prayer roll across the medina and forgetting what day it is. It's the kind of trip where "just a week in Marrakech" quietly turns into Fes, then the Sahara, then a coastal detour to Essaouira.

That elasticity is lovely right up until a check-in agent asks when you're leaving the country. Morocco is straightforward to enter for a lot of travellers, but the airline flying you in has its own rulebook, and the tidy answer is a booking that shows you departing. So here's what proof of onward travel really means for Morocco, who actually asks, and how to have it sorted before you're at the desk with a queue forming behind you.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Morocco

When Morocco admits you, it's granting a defined stay. Proof of onward travel is just evidence that you plan to leave inside it: a confirmed booking showing you departing Morocco, whether home or onward to Spain, Portugal, or your next stop, before your permitted time runs out.

It's separate from your entry permission. Being visa-free (or holding a visa, depending on your passport) means you're allowed in. Your onward booking shows you have a plan to leave. Morocco sits a short hop from Europe, and that closeness is exactly why airlines like to see that you're a genuine visitor with an exit, not a one-way arrival.

## The visa picture, briefly

Many nationalities, including citizens of the UK, the US, Canada, Australia, the EU, and others, can enter Morocco visa-free for tourism up to a set number of days. Travellers whose passports aren't on the visa-free list generally need to arrange a visa in advance, and Morocco has been developing an **electronic visa (e-visa)** for certain nationalities and residency situations.

Here's the honest bit: which passports are visa-free, for how long, and who's eligible for the e-visa all get updated, and they depend on your specific nationality and residence. So treat any number you read online, including here, as a reason to check rather than a fact to bank. Confirm your own requirement and permitted stay on the official Moroccan government or consulate channels before you book, and if you need an e-visa, use only the official portal. Plenty of lookalike sites mimic official ones and add a fee for nothing.

Whatever route applies, the onward-travel logic holds: your departure booking should show you leaving within the stay you've been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they're not the same

This is where people trip up, so it's worth splitting "will I get asked?" into its two real answers.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that surprises people. If an airline flies you to Morocco and you're refused entry, the airline eats the cost of flying you back, often with a fine attached. So the check-in agent is protecting the airline, not administering Moroccan law. They may ask to see a return or onward booking before handing you a boarding pass, and being visa-free doesn't always end the conversation. No onward proof, and you can find yourself unable to board a flight you've already paid for.

**At Moroccan immigration, on arrival.** Less predictable, more human. An officer at the border can ask about your plans, and a clear answer backed by a booking makes a short interaction shorter. It's more likely to come up if your stay is long, your answers are vague, or something about the trip prompts a second look.

The practical takeaway is the usual one: even if you're confident the immigration officer won't push, the airline might, so have something real ready to show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to Morocco

1. **Confirm your entry requirement and permitted stay** for your nationality, visa-free or visa, on the official Moroccan government or consulate channels.
2. **If you need a visa or e-visa, apply early** through the official portal only, and save the approval to your phone. Don't leave it to the last day.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Match your dates and name.** Make sure your onward booking sits inside your entry window, and that the name matches your passport exactly.
5. **Keep everything accessible**: entry approval (if any) and onward booking on your phone, plus a printout of the onward booking with its reference, ready before you reach the counter.

## FAQ

**Do I need onward travel if I'm visa-free for Morocco?**
Visa-free entry and onward proof answer two different questions. Being visa-free means you're allowed in without a visa; the onward booking shows you plan to leave within your permitted stay. Airlines in particular often want to see it regardless, so yes, have onward travel ready either way.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A fully paid ticket you don't intend to use just ties up money for the same job.

**How long can I stay in Morocco as a tourist?**
It depends on your nationality, and the rules get updated, so confirm your own allowance on official channels before you travel. Whatever the length, make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Can I show a ferry or bus booking instead of a flight?**
Onward travel doesn't have to be a flight if you're genuinely leaving overland or by sea, and a confirmed ferry to Spain can work. That said, airlines checking you in for the inbound flight are most comfortable with a clear, verifiable onward booking, so have something checkable ready.

**What if my plans past Morocco genuinely aren't decided?**
That's the common case, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation is for. You get real, verifiable proof to show at the gate without committing to a flight and a date you're not ready to lock in.
