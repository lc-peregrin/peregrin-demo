# Proof of Onward Travel for Taiwan: What You Actually Need in 2026

Taiwan has a reputation for being one of the easier places in Asia to just show up and get on with your trip. Friendly immigration, efficient airports, and a visa-free arrangement for a good chunk of the world's passports. That reputation is mostly earned. But "easy to enter" and "no questions asked" aren't the same thing, and the question that trips people up isn't about Taiwan's immigration desk at all. It's about the check-in counter back at your departure airport, where an airline agent wants to see that you're not planning to just... stay.

Here's what proof of onward travel actually means for Taiwan, who tends to ask for it, and how to have it sorted before you're the one holding up the queue.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Taiwan

When you're admitted into Taiwan, whether visa-free or on a visa, you're granted permission to remain for a set period, not an open-ended invitation. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you have a plan to leave within that window: a confirmed booking showing you departing Taiwan, whether home or onward somewhere else, before your permitted stay runs out.

That's different from your permission to enter in the first place. Your nationality and any visa or entry authorisation you hold get you through the door. Your onward booking is what shows you're not planning to overstay your welcome. Both matter, and they get checked by different people at different points.

## The entry picture, briefly (and hedged, because it moves)

A large number of passports can enter Taiwan visa-free for short stays, often somewhere in the range of two to three weeks, with a smaller list of nationalities needing a visa arranged in advance. Some visa-free travellers are also asked to register online before arrival. Exactly which category your passport falls into, how long your stay is, and whether any pre-registration applies, are all things that get adjusted from time to time by Taiwan's immigration authorities, so don't treat anything you read online, including this, as fixed.

Check your own nationality against Taiwan's official Bureau of Consular Affairs or National Immigration Agency channels close to your travel date, complete any online registration in advance if it applies to you, and make sure whatever onward booking you're carrying shows you leaving Taiwan inside your actual permitted stay, not just "at some point."

## Two checkpoints, and they behave differently

It helps to think of this as two separate hurdles, run by two different people with two different concerns.

**At check-in, before you even board.** This is the one that catches travellers out, because it's easy to assume it's Taiwan's problem to worry about, not the airline's. It's the other way round. If an airline flies you into Taiwan and you're refused entry for any reason, including having no clear way of leaving, the airline is on the hook to fly you back out at its own cost, and it can face a penalty too. So airlines lean cautious and ask to see onward or return travel before they'll print a boarding pass, well before you ever reach a Taiwanese immigration officer. No proof, and you can be stuck at your home airport with a ticket you can't use.

**At Taiwanese immigration, on arrival.** Officers here have a reputation for being fairly relaxed with travellers who look like ordinary tourists, but they can still ask about your onward plans, particularly on a longer stay or a one-way ticket. Having something ready to show turns that into a thirty-second formality instead of a longer conversation.

The upshot is the same wherever you're flying from: even if you're fairly confident immigration won't blink, the airline checking you in might, so carry something real.

## Step by step before you fly to Taiwan

1. **Confirm your entry category and permitted stay** for your exact nationality on Taiwan's official immigration channels, and check whether pre-arrival online registration applies to you.
2. **Complete any required online registration** ahead of time, on the official government site, and save your confirmation to your phone.
3. **Sort your onward travel:** a real flight you'll use, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation, showing you leaving Taiwan inside your permitted stay.
4. **Keep everything accessible:** entry documents, accommodation details, and your onward booking reference on your phone, plus a printed copy of the onward booking ready to hand over at check-in.

## FAQ

**Do I need onward travel if I'm entering Taiwan visa-free?**
Yes, generally. Visa-free entry answers whether you can come in; it doesn't answer how you're planning to leave. Airlines in particular tend to ask for onward or return travel regardless of your visa-free status, so it's worth having ready either way.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
What's generally expected is a confirmed onward booking that falls inside your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet exactly that, and because it's real and checkable, it does the job without you paying for airfare you don't need yet.

**How long can I stay in Taiwan visa-free?**
It depends entirely on your nationality, and the allowance (along with which passports qualify at all) can change. Confirm your own stay length on Taiwan's official immigration channels, and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Is online registration required before arrival?**
Some visa-free travellers to Taiwan need to complete an online arrival registration before their flight. Whether it applies to you depends on your nationality, so check the official government site ahead of time rather than assuming.

**Are Taiwanese immigration officers strict about onward tickets?**
Generally not aggressively so, and most tourist arrivals are straightforward. But officers can still ask, especially on longer stays or one-way tickets, and having a genuine onward booking ready to show keeps that a quick, easy exchange rather than an awkward one.
