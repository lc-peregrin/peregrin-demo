# Proof of Onward Travel for India: What You Actually Need in 2026

India is one of those trips that rearranges you a bit. You arrive with a plan and a spreadsheet, and somewhere around day three you're rewriting both from the back of a train, having decided that actually, Varanasi comes before Rajasthan now. That flexibility is half the joy of the place.

It's also exactly why the onward-travel question can catch people out. India's e-visa system is smooth and mostly online, but the tourist visa asks you to show a return or onward journey, and the airline flying you in tends to want to see it too. So here's what proof of onward travel really means for India, who actually asks, and how to have it sorted before you're standing at a check-in desk with a queue building behind you.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for India

When India grants you entry, it's giving you permission to stay for a defined window tied to your visa. Proof of onward travel is just evidence that you plan to leave inside that window: a confirmed booking showing you departing India, either home or onward to your next country, before your permitted stay ends.

It's separate from the visa itself. Your visa says you're allowed in. Your onward booking says you have a plan to leave. India is one of the countries where both matter, because the e-visa application form itself often asks about your travel dates and, for the tourist route, your intent to return or move on.

## The visa picture, briefly

Most travellers enter India on the **e-Tourist Visa**, applied for online before you fly through the official government portal. In its current form the tourist e-visa comes in different lengths (30-day, one-year, and five-year variants have all existed), with rules on how long a single stay can run and how many entries you get. There are separate e-visa categories for business, medical, and conference travel too.

Here's the honest part: the specifics shift, and they depend heavily on your nationality and which visa variant you hold. Stay lengths, entry counts, and even which passports are eligible for the e-visa get updated. So treat any number you read online, including here, as a prompt to go and check rather than gospel. Confirm your own eligibility and permitted stay on the official Indian government e-visa portal before you book anything, and apply only on that official site. Plenty of lookalike sites mimic it and tack on a fee for nothing.

Whichever variant you're on, the onward-travel logic holds: your departure booking should show you leaving within the stay you've been granted.

## Two checkpoints, and they're not the same

This trips people up, so it's worth being clear that "will I get asked?" has two separate answers.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that catches travellers off guard. Airlines carry the cost, and often a fine, if they fly someone to India who's then refused entry. So the check-in agent is protecting the airline, not administering Indian law. They may ask to see a return or onward booking before they hand you a boarding pass, and a valid e-visa on your phone doesn't always end the conversation. No onward proof, and you can find yourself unable to board a flight you've already paid for.

**At Indian immigration, on arrival.** Less predictable, more human. An officer can ask about your plans, and a clear answer backed by a booking makes a short interaction shorter. It tends to come up if your stay is long, your answers are vague, or your visa type invites the question.

The practical takeaway is the same one that applies almost everywhere: even if you're confident the immigration officer won't push, the airline might, so have something real ready to show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to India

1. **Confirm your visa route and permitted stay** for your nationality and visa variant, on the official Indian e-visa portal.
2. **Apply for your e-visa early** on the official government site, and save the approval (the ETA) to your phone. It isn't instant, so don't leave it to the last day.
3. **Sort your onward travel**: a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation showing you leaving within your permitted stay.
4. **Match your dates.** Make sure your onward booking sits inside your visa window, and that names match your passport exactly.
5. **Keep everything accessible**: e-visa approval and onward booking on your phone, plus a printout of the onward booking with its reference, ready before you reach the counter.

## FAQ

**Do I really need onward travel if my e-visa is approved?**
Your e-visa and your onward proof answer two different questions. The visa gives you permission to enter; the onward booking shows you plan to leave within it. India's tourist route leans on that intent, and airlines in particular often want to see it, so yes, have onward travel ready even with an approved e-visa.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A fully paid ticket you don't intend to use just ties up money for the same job.

**How long can I stay in India on a tourist e-visa?**
It depends on your nationality and which e-visa variant you hold, and the rules get updated. Short and long variants have both existed, sometimes with limits on continuous stay. Confirm your own allowance on the official portal, and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Where do I apply for the e-visa?**
Only on the official Indian government e-visa portal. Numerous third-party sites copy its look and charge a markup, so check the web address carefully before you enter any details.

**What if my plans past India genuinely aren't decided?**
That's the common case, and it's exactly what a genuine held reservation is for. You get real, verifiable proof to show at the gate without committing to a flight and a date you're not ready to commit to.
