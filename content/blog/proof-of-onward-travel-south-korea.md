# Proof of Onward Travel for South Korea: What You Actually Need in 2026

South Korea takes its border seriously, in the polite, exceptionally organised way it takes most things. There's an electronic travel authorisation for a lot of visa-free visitors, a slick arrival process, and immigration officers who tend to ask a question or two. So when you're flying into Incheon and someone, either the airline at check-in or the officer on arrival, wants to know how and when you're leaving, it pays to have a clean answer ready rather than a shrug and a "not sure yet."

Here's what proof of onward travel actually means for South Korea, who gets asked, and how to have it sorted before you're standing at a counter wishing you'd thought about it earlier.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for South Korea

When you're admitted to South Korea, whether visa-free or on a visa, you get permission to stay for a defined period. Proof of onward travel is simply evidence that you intend to leave within it: a confirmed booking that shows you departing the country, home or on to somewhere else, before your permitted stay ends.

It's a different thing from your permission to enter. Your nationality, your K-ETA where required, or your visa is what gets you in. Your onward booking is what shows you have a plan to leave. South Korea's system, and the airlines flying you there, tend to care about both.

## The entry picture, briefly

Many nationalities can visit South Korea visa-free for tourism for a set number of days, and a lot of those visitors are asked to hold a **K-ETA** (an electronic travel authorisation applied for online before departure), though the exact requirement, who's exempt, and how long it stays valid have all shifted more than once and can change again. Some passports need a visa arranged in advance instead. There's also an arrival process and, for some, an entry card.

Because all of that moves, treat any figure or rule you read as a starting point, not gospel. Check your own nationality against South Korea's official immigration and K-ETA channels close to your travel date, sort the K-ETA in advance if it applies to you (don't leave it to the airport), and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside whatever stay your passport is actually granted. The onward-travel logic doesn't change with the route: your departure booking should fall within your permitted stay.

## Two checkpoints, and they behave differently

Worth separating these, because they're run by different people with different worries.

**At check-in, before you board.** This is the one that trips travellers up. If an airline carries you to South Korea and you're refused entry, the airline has to fly you back out at its own expense and can be penalised as well. So airlines protect themselves by asking to see onward or return travel before they issue a boarding pass. A valid K-ETA on your phone doesn't get you around this, because it answers a different question. No onward proof, and you can be stuck at the departure airport with a boarding pass you can't get.

**At South Korean immigration, on arrival.** More likely to come up here than in some countries. Korean officers are thorough and may ask about the purpose of your visit, where you're staying, and when you're leaving, particularly on a one-way ticket or a longer trip. A departure booking ready to show keeps that exchange short and easy.

The rule of thumb holds: even if you think immigration will wave you through, the airline may not, so carry something real you can show at the gate.

## Step by step before you fly to South Korea

1. **Confirm your entry route and permitted stay** for your exact nationality on South Korea's official immigration channels, and check whether you need a K-ETA or a visa in advance.
2. **Apply for your K-ETA early** if it applies to you, on the official site rather than a lookalike that adds a markup, and save the approval to your phone.
3. **Sort your onward travel:** a real flight you'll take, a refundable ticket, or a genuine verifiable reservation, showing you leaving South Korea inside your permitted stay.
4. **Keep everything accessible:** K-ETA or visa, accommodation details, and onward booking on your phone, with a printout of the onward booking and its reference ready at check-in.

## FAQ

**Do I need onward travel if my K-ETA is approved?**
Your K-ETA and your onward proof answer two different questions. The K-ETA is permission to travel to Korea and seek entry; the onward booking shows you plan to leave. Airlines often want both, so have onward travel ready even with an approved K-ETA.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real reference is designed to meet that, and it's real and checkable, which is the whole point. A purchased ticket you won't use just ties up money.

**How long can I stay in South Korea visa-free?**
It depends on your nationality; visa-free visitors get a set number of days that varies by passport, and some travellers need a visa instead. Confirm your own allowance on South Korea's official channels and make sure your onward booking shows you leaving inside it.

**Where do I apply for the K-ETA?**
Only on South Korea's official K-ETA site. Third-party sites copy the look and add a fee, so check the URL carefully before you enter anything or pay.

**Are Korean immigration officers strict about onward tickets?**
They're thorough, and it's common to be asked about your plans, accommodation, and departure, especially on a one-way ticket. It's nothing to worry about if you have a genuine onward booking ready to show; it just turns a few questions into a quick formality.
