# Flight Reservation for a Schengen Visa: Why You Should Not Buy the Ticket Yet (2026)

Applying for a Schengen visa comes with a checklist as long as your arm. Passport, photos, insurance, bank statements, accommodation, and somewhere in the middle of it all, proof of your flights. This is where a lot of people make an expensive mistake. They see "flight itinerary" on the list, panic, and go buy a full round trip to Europe before they have any idea whether the visa will actually be granted.

Do not do that. Here is what the consulates actually ask for, why several of them will literally tell you not to buy the ticket yet, and how to get exactly what they want in a couple of minutes.

<!-- INLINE IMAGE: Schengen visa sticker in a passport. alt: "A Schengen visa sticker inside a passport" -->

## What the visa office actually wants

For a short stay Schengen visa you have to show your intended travel: a round trip flight booking that lines up with your dates and the country you are applying through. The key word is booking. Almost every consulate asks for a reservation or an itinerary, not a paid ticket. They want to see a real, dated plan they can check, not proof that you have already spent a thousand euros.

It makes sense from their side. Visa decisions take time and they are never guaranteed. If they made everyone buy a ticket up front, they would be forcing applicants to gamble a big chunk of money on an outcome nobody can promise. So the whole system is built around reservations.

## Do not just take my word for it. Take theirs.

This is not a loophole or a clever workaround. The people who decide your visa are the ones telling you to bring a reservation and hold off on the ticket. A few, word for word, straight from their own official guidance:

- The Royal Norwegian Embassy: "Do not buy the ticket until visa has been granted."
- [Germany's Federal Foreign Office](https://singapur.diplo.de/sg-en/service/05-visaeinreise/2455416-2455416): "It is recommended that at no time a flight ticket should be purchased until a visa has been granted." It even spells out that "a flight reservation or printout of your flight itinerary (NO booked ticket is needed)."
- [Finland's Ministry for Foreign Affairs](https://finlandabroad.fi/documents/3608726/0/Visa+Requirements_ENG.pdf): "Flight reservation (roundtrip). Please note: It is not recommended to purchase the tickets before receiving a visa decision."
- [Denmark's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://usa.um.dk/en/travel-and-residence/practical-information/visa/document-checklists): "The Consulate General recommend you not to purchase a flight ticket before a visa has been granted."
- [Belgium](https://ireland.diplomatie.belgium.be/sites/default/files/files/schengen_notice_visas_c_version_12_2022.pdf) tells applicants to check the status of their application "before buying your ticket and making any financial commitments."

When Norway, Germany, Finland, Denmark and Belgium all independently say the same thing, it is not a grey area. A reservation is the sensible, expected document. Buying the ticket early is the mistake.

**Ask an airline or agent to hold a booking.** Some will, some will not, and the holds tend to expire fast or come with strings attached. Fiddly.

## Getting your documents right

1. Confirm which consulate you apply through, usually your main destination, or your first point of entry if you are splitting time evenly.
2. Get a round trip flight reservation matching your planned dates and that country, with a verifiable booking reference.
3. Line it up with the rest: travel insurance covering the Schengen area, accommodation bookings, bank statements, and your cover letter.
4. Do not buy the actual ticket until the visa is stamped in your passport. Then book with confidence.

## FAQ

**Do I really need a flight reservation for a Schengen visa?**
Yes, essentially always. Consulates want to see your intended travel, and a reservation or itinerary is the standard document. Several consulates specifically say a booked ticket is not needed.

**Reservation or ticket, what is the difference?**
A ticket is paid for and issued. A reservation is a real, held booking with a reference, but the fare is not paid. For the visa application, the reservation is what they ask for.

**Will they check if it is real?**
They can, and sometimes do, by looking the reference up with the airline. That is exactly why a genuine, verifiable reservation matters, and a fake one is dangerous.

**When should I actually buy the flights?**
After the visa is approved. That is the whole reason the consulates tell you to wait.

**What if my visa is refused?**
Then you will be very glad you did not spend hundreds on a ticket. You have lost only the small cost of the reservation, not the airfare.
