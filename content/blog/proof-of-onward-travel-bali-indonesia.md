# Proof of Onward Travel for Bali and Indonesia: What You Actually Need in 2026

Bali runs on a certain kind of optimism. You book a one way flight, promise yourself you will figure out the rest when you get there, and picture yourself on a scooter with a coconut before lunch. Lovely plan. The problem is that the check-in desk does not share your vibe, and neither does Indonesian immigration. Both want to see how you intend to leave before they will let you arrive.

That thing they want is called proof of onward travel. Here is what it actually means, who gets asked, and how to keep everyone happy without dropping real money on a flight you have no intention of taking.

## What proof of onward travel actually means

Most people land in Bali on a Visa on Arrival, or its online cousin the e-VOA. Both cost around 500,000 rupiah, roughly 35 US dollars, and both give you 30 days. You can extend once for another 30, so 60 days is the ceiling. More than 90 nationalities qualify.

Because that stay is capped, Indonesia wants evidence that you plan to leave inside it. That evidence is a confirmed booking showing you flying out of the country, home or onward somewhere else, before your welcome runs out. A return or onward ticket is a listed entry requirement, plain and simple.

While you are at it, note that Bali also charges a separate tourist levy, paid online or on arrival. Different thing from onward travel, but you may as well handle both at once.

## Who actually gets asked

Assume you need it if you are arriving on a VOA or e-VOA, on visa free entry where that applies, or on a longer visa if an airline decides to ask. Hold a proper long stay visa with clear dates, like a remote worker permit, and immigration usually relaxes. The airlines do not. They run their own checks no matter what visa you carry, and that is where most people get caught.

## The two checkpoints, and why the airline is the one to fear

There are two places this gets checked, and they are nothing alike.

The first is check-in, before you have left home. This is the one that actually stops people. If an airline flies you to Denpasar and Indonesia turns you back, the airline eats the cost of flying you home and can be fined too. So they cover themselves by asking to see an onward booking before they hand you a boarding pass. No onward proof, no boarding, and it happens at your departure gate long before Bali is even on the horizon.

The second is Indonesian immigration on arrival. An officer can ask for your onward booking. Whether they do is a coin toss that depends on the officer and the day. The requirement is real, and you do not want to be the one person who cannot show anything.

Short version: even if you doubt a Bali officer will ask, your airline very well might. Have something ready for the gate.

## Your options, worst to best

**Buy a fully refundable ticket.** It works, but flexible fares cost a fortune and refunds can crawl back over weeks. If your plans might shift, and in Bali they always do, you have parked real money on a seat you may never use.

## Getting ready, step by step

1. Confirm your visa route (VOA or e-VOA, 30 days, extend once to 60) and that your nationality qualifies.
2. To skip the airport queue, apply for the e-VOA online in advance on the official immigration site.
3. Sort your onward travel: a real flight, a genuinely refundable ticket, or a verifiable reservation showing you leaving inside your window.
4. Pay the Bali tourist levy online, or be ready to at arrival.
5. Keep it all reachable: onward booking and reference on your phone, printed too if you like a backup, ready for check-in and immigration.

## FAQ

**Will I definitely get asked for it in Bali?**
Not always at immigration, it varies. But airlines are consistent at check-in, so carry something no matter what you expect the officer to do.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a bought ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking inside your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation meets that. Never rely on a fake.

**How long can I stay on a VOA?**
30 days, extendable once for another 30, so 60 total. Your onward booking should show you leaving inside that.

**Is the tourist levy the same as proof of onward travel?**
No, separate Bali tourism fee. You may need both.

**What if I turn up with nothing?**
Worst case the airline will not board you, or immigration gives you a hard time. Five minutes of prep and the whole problem is gone.
