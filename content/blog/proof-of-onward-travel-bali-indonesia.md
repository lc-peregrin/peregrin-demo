# Proof of Onward Travel for Bali & Indonesia: What You Actually Need in 2026

Bali is one of the world's most popular destinations for tourists and digital nomads alike — and one requirement trips up a surprising number of arrivals: proof of onward travel. Indonesia expects visitors to show they'll leave within their permitted stay, and airlines enforce it strictly at check-in. The good news is it's easy to satisfy once you know what's being asked for.

This guide covers what proof of onward travel means for Bali and the rest of Indonesia, who needs it, how it's enforced, and the practical ways to show it — including how to do so without spending hundreds of dollars on a flight you may never take.

## What "proof of onward travel" means for Indonesia

Most visitors enter Bali on a **Visa on Arrival (VOA)** or its online version, the **e-VOA**. Both cost around IDR 500,000 (about US$35), grant a **30-day stay**, and can be **extended once for another 30 days** — a 60-day maximum. Over 90 nationalities are eligible. Because that permission is time-limited, Indonesian immigration wants evidence you intend to leave within it, and that evidence is your **proof of onward travel**: a confirmed booking showing you departing Indonesia — home or onward to another country — before your stay runs out.

A return or onward ticket is listed as a standard entry requirement. Note that Bali also charges a separate **tourist levy** (paid online or on arrival) — a different requirement from onward travel, but worth handling at the same time.

## Who needs it

Be ready to show proof of onward travel if you're entering Indonesia on a VOA or e-VOA, on visa-free entry (where available), or in many cases on a longer visa if an airline asks. If you hold a longer-stay visa (for example a remote-worker or social-cultural visa) with clear validity, immigration is usually less concerned — but airlines run their own checks regardless of your visa, and that's where most travellers actually hit the requirement.

## How it's enforced — the airline is the strict one

There are two places this can be checked, and they behave differently.

**At check-in, before you board.** Airlines are the strict enforcers. Under international rules, if a carrier flies you to Indonesia and you're refused entry, the airline is responsible for flying you back — and can be fined. So many airlines ask to see an onward or return booking before they'll issue your boarding pass. No onward proof, no boarding — and this happens at your departure airport, long before you reach Bali.

**At Indonesian immigration, on arrival.** Officers can ask for proof of onward travel. Enforcement varies by officer and day, but the requirement is real, and you don't want to be the traveller who's asked when you can't show it.

The practical takeaway: even if you doubt a Bali officer will ask, the airline very well might — so have something ready to show at the gate.

## The ways to satisfy the requirement

**Buy a fully refundable ticket.** It works, but genuinely flexible refundable fares are expensive, and refunds come with conditions and delays. If your plans might change — or you're only booking to satisfy the requirement — you're tying up real money for a seat you may never use.

**Book a cheap real onward flight you'll take.** If you already know your next stop (Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Australia), booking that leg early is the cleanest solution — a real ticket you'll actually use.

A reservation is a genuine airline booking with a real booking reference (PNR) that can be verified against the airline's system, held under the fare's own rules without the airfare being charged. ## Step by step: getting ready to fly to Bali

1. Confirm your visa route (VOA or e-VOA; 30 days, extendable once to 60) and that your nationality is eligible.
2. If you want to skip the airport queue, apply for the **e-VOA** online in advance at the official immigration portal.
3. Sort your onward travel — a real onward flight, a fully refundable ticket, or a verifiable reservation showing you leaving Indonesia within your permitted stay.
4. Pay the **Bali tourist levy** online (or be ready to at arrival).
5. Keep everything accessible — your onward booking and its reference on your phone and, ideally, printed, ready at check-in and immigration.

## FAQ

**Will I definitely be asked for proof of onward travel for Bali?**
Not always at immigration — it varies. But airlines check consistently at check-in, so always have something ready regardless of what you expect the officer to do.

**Does a reservation count, or do I need a paid ticket?**
The requirement is a confirmed onward booking within your permitted stay. A genuine, verifiable reservation with a real booking reference is designed to meet that. Never rely on a fake or edited document.

**How long can I stay in Bali on a VOA?**
30 days, extendable once for another 30 — a 60-day maximum. Your onward booking should show you leaving within that window.

**Is the tourist levy the same as proof of onward travel?**
No — it's a separate Bali tourism fee. You may need both.

**What happens if I arrive with no onward proof?**
At worst the airline denies boarding, or immigration questions you. A few minutes of preparation removes the risk entirely.
